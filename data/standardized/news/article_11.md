# IELTS Writing Time Management & Exam Strategies: From Brainstorming to 5-Minute Proofreading

The 60-minute IELTS Writing test requires disciplined time management and systematic execution. Poor pacing is the single most common reason why capable candidates fail to achieve their target band score.

## 1. Golden Pacing Rule: Complete Task 2 First

* **Why Task 2 First?**
  * Task 2 carries **66.7% (two-thirds)** of the total Writing marks, whereas Task 1 accounts for only 33.3%.
  * Writing 250+ thoughtful words requires high cognitive stamina. Completing Task 2 while your mind is fresh guarantees that the highest-scoring component receives the attention it warrants.

## 2. The 40-Minute Task 2 Master Timeline

### Phase 1: Planning & Brainstorming (5 - 7 Minutes)
* **Step 1: Analyze the Prompt Carefully (2 mins)**
  * Identify the Topic (What is the core issue?), the Micro-topic (What specific aspect is asked?), and the Instruction (Is it an opinion, discussion, problem-solution, or direct question?).
* **Step 2: Decide Your Position (1 min)**
  * Formulate your clear, unambiguous thesis before writing a single word.
* **Step 3: Brainstorm Supporting Arguments using the 'Perspective Method' (3 mins)**
  * Analyze the issue through multiple lenses: *Economic, Social, Psychological, Educational, Environmental*.
  * Select only the **2 strongest arguments**. Quality of elaboration is heavily rewarded over quantity of superficial points.

### Phase 2: Drafting the Essay (28 - 30 Minutes)
* **Introduction (5 mins):** 2 sentences total (Sentence 1: Paraphrase prompt; Sentence 2: Clear thesis + main arguments preview).
* **Body Paragraph 1 (10 mins):** 4-5 sentences using P.E.E.L (Point, Explain, Example, Link).
* **Body Paragraph 2 (10 mins):** 4-5 sentences using P.E.E.L for the second core argument.
* **Conclusion (3-4 mins):** 1-2 sentences restating the thesis and summarizing key points without introducing new ideas.

### Phase 3: The Critical 5-Minute Proofreading Checklist (3 - 5 Minutes)
* Almost all candidates make 3 to 6 avoidable slips when writing under exam pressure. Catching these errors can raise your Grammatical Accuracy score from Band 6 to Band 7.5:
  1. **Subject-Verb Agreement:** Check singular/plural consistency (e.g., *the government needs* vs *governments need*).
  2. **Articles (a, an, the):** Check for missing definite articles before specific nouns or incorrect use before uncountable nouns.
  3. **Plural vs Singular Nouns:** Check words that end in -s.
  4. **Punctuation & Comma Splices:** Ensure independent clauses are joined by coordinating conjunctions or semi-colons, not comma splices.
  5. **Spelling & Word Endings:** Double check tricky academic words (*environment, definitely, modern, government, independent*).

## 3. The 20-Minute Task 1 Master Timeline

* **Minutes 0 - 3 (Analyze & Find Overview):** Circle the highest point, lowest point, major changes, and similarities across categories. Decide on the 2 key features for the overview.
* **Minutes 3 - 6 (Introduction & Overview):** Write the paraphrased introduction and the concise 2-sentence overview.
* **Minutes 6 - 16 (Detail Body Paragraphs):** Write Body 1 and Body 2, grouping data logically and citing exact figures and units.
* **Minutes 16 - 20 (Verify Figures & Data):** Ensure you haven't misread the axes, dates, units (thousands vs millions), or percentages.
